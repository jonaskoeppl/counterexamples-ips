# If one reversible measure exists, is every stationary measure reversible?

**Status:** open (for stochastic Ising models in $d \ge 3$)

## Question

Let a spin system on $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^d}$ have at least one reversible measure. Is every stationary measure reversible? Under which hypotheses (finite range, strictly positive rates, translation-invariant rates, spin system vs. conservative dynamics)?

## Known

- Holley (1971): for stochastic Ising models, every translation-invariant stationary measure is Gibbs, in all $d$.
- Holley and Stroock (1977): for $d \le 2$, every stationary measure is Gibbs, hence reversible.
- Liggett (1985), Ch. IV: the case $d \ge 3$ is listed as open.

## Cautionary example

Nearest-neighbour ASEP on $\mathbb{Z}$: blocking measures are reversible, while Bernoulli product measures are stationary but not reversible. Written up in [entry 0001](../entries/0001-asep-blocking-vs-product-measures/).
