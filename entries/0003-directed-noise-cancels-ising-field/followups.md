# Directed noise can destroy ergodicity — follow-up questions

1. **Bérard–Dembin–Marêché, Section 1.5:** does every monotone ergodic IPS admit an arbitrarily small perturbation that is exponentially ergodic? This entry does not answer it: for the $L$ constructed here, $L_{\varepsilon'}$ is ergodic for every $\varepsilon' \neq \varepsilon$. — `open`
2. **Arbitrarily small noise.** Is there a monotone ergodic $L$ such that $L_\varepsilon$ is non-ergodic for arbitrarily small $\varepsilon > 0$, or for all $\varepsilon$ in an interval? Here the bad set is the single point $\{\varepsilon\}$, because the non-ergodic set of the Ising model ($h = 0$, $\beta > \beta_c$) is a line. A model whose non-ergodic region has non-empty interior, such as Toom's NEC rule with biased noise (Toom 1980; continuous time: Bramson–Gray 1991), might give an interval. — `open` (references to be checked)
3. **Rate of convergence of $L$.** Is $L$ (constant down-rate, field $h > 0$, $\beta > \beta_c$) exponentially ergodic? This is expected, but with a relaxation time that blows up as $h \downarrow 0$ (droplet nucleation; cf. Schonmann 1994 for Glauber dynamics). — `expected` (to be checked)
4. **Dimension one.** The construction needs a phase transition, hence $d \ge 2$. Does the naive generalisation hold in $d = 1$? For nearest-neighbour attractive systems with positive rates every such system is ergodic (Gray 1982), so a one-dimensional counterexample would need range $\ge 2$ or vanishing rates. — `open`
5. **Symmetric noise.** Does adding noise in *both* directions ($c_0 + \varepsilon$, $c_1 + \varepsilon$) preserve ergodicity of monotone systems? Here the trick fails: $c_1 + \varepsilon$ is no longer proportional to $e^{2\beta S_x}$, so the perturbed system is not a stochastic Ising model. — `open` (see `problems/noise-preserves-ergodicity.md`)
6. **Choosing the direction.** For a monotone ergodic $L$, is at least one of the two directed perturbations (noise towards $0$ or towards $1$) ergodic for all $\varepsilon > 0$? In this example, noise towards $+1$ reinforces the field, but the perturbed process is again not reversible. — `open`
