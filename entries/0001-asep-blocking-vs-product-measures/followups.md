# One reversible measure, but a non-reversible stationary measure: exclusion processes — follow-up questions

1. Positive-rate, finite-range, translation-invariant spin systems on $\mathbb{Z}^d$, $d \ge 3$: if there is a reversible measure, is every stationary measure reversible? (This is the Holley–Stroock question.) — `open`
2. Does the claim hold for exclusion processes if the reversible measure is required to be translation invariant? For irreducible translation-invariant $p(\cdot,\cdot)$ on $\mathbb{Z}^d$, $\nu_\rho$ ($0<\rho<1$) is reversible iff $p$ is symmetric, and then every stationary measure is a mixture of the $\nu_\rho$, hence reversible. — `known` (Liggett 1985, Ch. VIII; to be written up)
3. Describe all stationary measures of ASEP on $\mathbb{Z}$. For nearest-neighbour ASEP the extremal ones should be the $\nu_\rho$ and the blocking measures. Which of them are reversible? — `known` (Liggett 1976; Bramson–Liggett–Mountford, *Ann. Probab.* 30 (2002), for general one-dimensional exclusion; references to be checked)
4. Check the full-support example in $d \ge 2$ (Remark in `digest.md`) and describe its stationary measures. — `open`
5. Is there a *non-conservative* process (no conservation law, but possibly with zero rates) with a reversible measure and a non-reversible stationary measure? Which minimal hypothesis rules this out? — `open`
